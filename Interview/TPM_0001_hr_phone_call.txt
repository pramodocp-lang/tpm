7. Tell me about a challenging project.

Situation:
One of the most challenging projects I worked on was delivering a 
major release for our Educational CRM platform. The release included multiple features such as enhancements to 
Student Admission Management, Counsellor Dashboard, and Application Tracking. 
We had a strict deadline because the release needed to go live before the new admission cycle.

Task:
As the Technical Project Manager, my responsibility was to ensure the release was delivered on time 
while coordinating engineering, QA, product, DevOps, and business stakeholders. 
The biggest challenge was managing multiple cross-team dependencies, changing business priorities, and resolving technical blockers 
without impacting the release schedule.

Action:
I started by breaking the work into clear milestones and identifying the critical path. 
I tracked all work items in Jira and maintained visibility through sprint boards and dashboards.

I facilitated daily stand-ups, backlog refinement sessions, sprint planning, 
and weekly stakeholder meetings to ensure everyone had the latest updates.

Whenever risks or blockers were identified, I recorded them in the RAID log, assigned owners, 
and followed up until they were resolved. For example, when a dependency delayed one feature, 
I worked with the Product Owner to reprioritize the backlog so the team could continue working on other high-priority items instead of waiting.

I also maintained transparent communication with stakeholders by sharing progress, risks, 
mitigation plans, and any schedule impacts early, ensuring there were no surprises.

Result:
As a result, we successfully delivered the release on schedule with high quality. The production deployment was smooth,
critical defects were minimal, and the business was able to start the admission cycle on time. 
The project strengthened stakeholder confidence, and the structured coordination process we followed became a model for future releases.

11. How do you handle project risks?

I proactively identify risks during the project planning phase and maintain a RAID log to track them.

I assess each risk based on its probability and impact, and then create appropriate mitigation or contingency plans.

I regularly review the risks with the project team and key stakeholders and assign clear owners and target dates.

If a risk materializes into an issue, I take immediate action, communicate the impact to stakeholders, and work with the team to resolve it.

This helps me minimize project delays and keep the project on track.


Example 1 — Key Developer Unavailable

One example was when a key developer became unavailable during an important sprint. 
I identified this as a delivery risk and assessed its potential impact on the sprint timeline.

I discussed the situation with the Engineering Manager and identified another team member 
who could take over the critical tasks. We also prioritized the backlog and focused on the high-priority items first.

As a result, we were able to minimize the impact and keep the sprint on track.



Example 2 — Third-Party API Dependency

Another example was when our project depended on a third-party payment API, 
and there was a risk that the API integration could be delayed.

I discussed the dependency with the technical team, tracked it in the RAID log, 
and agreed on a mitigation plan. We requested the API documentation and test environment early and also planned a 
mock API so that development could continue while the external dependency was being resolved.

This helped us avoid blocking the development team and reduced the risk of delaying the release.



Example 3 — Changing Business Requirements

One risk I have handled was frequent changes in business requirements during development. 
I identified the risk of scope creep and potential impact on the timeline.

I worked with the Product Owner to assess each change based on business priority, 
effort, and project impact. Critical changes were prioritized, while non-critical changes were moved to the next sprint.

This helped us control scope and maintain the committed delivery timeline.



Example 4 — UAT Delay

Another example was a potential UAT delay because the business users were not available for testing.

I identified this as a delivery risk and discussed it with the business stakeholders. 
We agreed on the UAT schedule in advance and assigned specific users for testing.

I also tracked UAT progress regularly and escalated any pending feedback. 
This helped us complete UAT on time and avoid impacting the release.



Example 5 — Production Release Risk

Before an important production release, we identified a risk related to a critical feature that had limited testing time.

I worked with QA and engineering to prioritize the critical test scenarios and ensure that 
all high-severity defects were addressed before release.

We also prepared a rollback plan in case any critical issue occurred after deployment. 
This reduced the risk associated with the production release.



Example 6 — Resource Availability

In one project, I identified a resource availability risk because multiple team members 
were planned for different projects at the same time.

I discussed the resource allocation with the Engineering Manager and reviewed the 
project priorities. We redistributed some tasks and 
cross-trained team members to reduce dependency on specific individuals.

As a result, the project continued without a significant impact on the timeline.
